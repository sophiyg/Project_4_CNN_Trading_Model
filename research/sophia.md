All three are semiconductor stock.

**QCOM:**
* Qualcomm: Designs chips and licenses wireless technology for smartphones and connected devices
* Sells:
  * Snapdragon processors for phones, PCs, and automotive systems
  * Modems and wireless connectivity chips
  * Licensing of cellular technology patents 
* Measured through Sum-of-the-Parts (SOTP): QCT technologies + QTL licensing
* Key Performance Indicators:
  * Trailing P/E Ratio: 12.24x. This indicates that the market values QCOm as a mature cyclical stock rather than a premium growth firm.
  * Trailing 12-Month Revenue: $44.28B. Investors track this absolute figure to see if diversification efforts are successfully growing the aggregate business.
  * Revenue Growth Year-over-Year (YoY): 14.2%. QCOM navigated a severe smartphone inventory correction over the last two years, and now investors look at this rebounding double-digit top-line growth to confirm a cyclical recovery in handsets.
  * Diluted Earnings Per Share (EPS): $5.06. "bottom line" metric that is used to see how efficiently revenue growth is converted into per-share profitability.
  * Automotive Segment Revenue Growth: monitoring QCOm’s progress towards achieving a target of over $8B in automotive revenue by 2029.
  * IoT: Internet of Technology is Qualcomm Technologies' comprehensive portfolio of hardware chipsets, edge AI platforms, software frameworks, and end-to-end services. IoT revenue expansion helps to gauge QCOm’ penetration into personal computing market
  * QTL Operating Margin: normally operates at an 80%+ margin. Any degradation directly destroys QCOM’s profit (licensing is their main source of revenue).
* Movements:
  * Pullback driven by rising input costs and macro-handset saturation
  * Positive triggers caused by automotive node expansion and high-profile satellite partnership with SpaceX
* Investor Sentiment:
  * Cheap: viewed as fundamentally undervalued because of cyclical friction. 

**INTC**
* Intel: Designs and manufactures its own processors, primarily for PCs and data centers
* Sells:
  * CPUs for PCs and laptops
  * Server processors for data centers
  * Semiconductors for external customers
* Key Performance Indicators:
  * Trailing P/E Ratio: -22.02x. Because Intel is recovering from deep net-income losses, it currently has a negative trailing P/E. For this company, investors are ignoring traditional trailing earnings multiples here and look toward multi-year normalization.
  * Trailing 12-Month Revenue: $59.89B. Gross revenue stability is watched to ensure customers aren't fully migrating to rivals like AMD or Nvidia.
  * Gross Profit & Margin: At $24.05B (roughly a 40.1% gross margin), this is the number one operational metric investors watch. Historically, Intel enjoyed 60%+ gross margins. Expansion or contraction in this number directly moves the stock.
  * Net Profit Margin: -20.09%. Due to heavy impairment charges, restructuring costs, and capital expenditures for factory buildouts, the net margin remains sharply in the negative.
  * The metric that drives future valuation projections is the dollar value of contract manufacturing commitments Intel secures from external chip designers (like Microsoft, Amazon, or even competing chipmakers).
* Movements:
  * Restructuring volatility. This was seen with massive rallies up 120%+. Cratered multiple times due to collapsing core PC client segment and factory losses
* Sentiment:
  * Expensive on earnings but cheap on assets. 

**TSM**
* Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company (TSMC): Manufactures chips designed by other companies, including Apple, Nvidia, AMD, and Qualcomm.
* Sells:
  * Advanced chips for other semiconductor companies
  * Packaging for AI processors
* Key Performance Indicators:
  * Trailing P/E Ratio: 25.21x. This is slightly higher than its historical median (~22.6x),  pricing in robust multi-year demand for AI accelerators from Nvidia and AMD.
  * Forward P/E Ratio: ~16x to 18.8x (depending on forward 2027 estimates). This compression shows that earnings growth is expected to quickly outpace the current stock price
  * Gross Profit Margin: 66.4%. This is a critical metric for TSMC. Historically, management aimed for "53% or higher," but the steep ramp-up of 3nm and pricing power on CoWoS advanced packaging has expanded margins to elite levels.
  * Operating Margin: 58.7%. Investors track this to ensure overhead and the expansion of international fabs (like Arizona and Japan) aren't eating into core profitability.
* Movements:
  * AI Capacity constraints: caused the stock to rally over 30% on a structural layout shift towards High performance computing and data centers
  * Minor pullbacks due to tech sector cooling and 2nm node deployment costs
* Sentiment:
  * Historically cheap. Has a high market tag but with roughly 25x P/E and 16 forward PE, analysts view this as low given their monopoly over next gen processing units and massive contract pricing leverage.

**What our agent should look at (for these three companies specifically):**

1. Advanced Packaging Capacity and Node Progress

Track leading-edge manufacturing milestones, such as advanced node revenue share (3nm/2nm), CoWoS packaging capacity, and the high-performance computing mix. For TSMC, this factor dictates its immense pricing power; for Qualcomm, it determines if they can secure enough supply allocation to launch premium chips; and for Intel, it measures whether their turnaround foundry is successfully catching up to competitors.

2. CapEx Efficiency and Factory Utilization Rates

Capital Expenditures (CapEx) guidance, factory utilization percentages, and free cash flow margins help evaluate how efficiently a company deploys capital. Chip manufacturing is the most capital-intensive industry on earth, meaning that revenue growth can still destroy shareholder value if factories run empty or over-budget. When TSMC raises CapEx, it signals guaranteed, pre-booked customer demand, whereas when Intel raises CapEx, it accelerates immediate cash burn and strains the balance sheet.

3. Gross Margin and Operating Efficiency

Should prioritize tracking gross profit margin and operating margin over simple revenue growth to accurately capture structural profitability. This should be looked into because for TSMC, a gross margin climbing past 66% signals an absolute monopoly on AI chip production while for Intel, watching gross margin recover toward its historical 60% baseline tracks the success of its factory turnaround. For Qualcomm, a stable 80%+ operating margin in its QTL segment ensures the company remains an elite cash machine regardless of smartphone sales volumes.
